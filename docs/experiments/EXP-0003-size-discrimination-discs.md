# EXP-0003: echoes of the size-discrimination discs

Status: pre-registered 2026-11-15; run 2026-11-16; **corrected 2026-11-17**. Results
are below the protocol, which has not been rewritten. Two dated amendments record how the
decision rule was read and a defect found in the level metric.

## Question

Can the production engine (geometric acoustics with edge diffraction, decision 2)
reproduce the echo levels of the discs used in the size-discrimination task of the
training study we take as our template (literature A3), over the distances that task
uses?

## Why this workload

It replaces invented test sizes with the stimuli of a published, validated training
task. Literature A3 used a 25.4 cm reference disc against comparison discs of 5.1, 9.0,
13.5, 17.5 and 22.9 cm diameter, starting at 33 cm and moving 33 cm further after two
sessions at 90% correct; experts worked at 100 cm. Literature B2 measured detection of a
17.5 cm disc at 100 cm.

## Workload (fixed)

- Six rigid discs of diameter 5.1, 9.0, 13.5, 17.5, 22.9 and 25.4 cm.
- Three distances: 33, 66 and 99 cm. Eighteen cases.
- Each disc faces the listener, centred straight ahead; source and receiver coincide.
- Simplifications, stated so they are not mistaken for the real task: the study's discs
  were foam board on a pole, two at a time, above and below ear height.

## Reference (fixed)

The wave solver of `soundsight.acoustics.reference.fdtd`, at 12 or more cells per disc
radius and 10 or more cells per wavelength at 4 kHz, with the sub-cell edge correction.

## Metrics and thresholds (fixed)

1. **Level.** For each of the 18 cases, echo level over 2 to 4 kHz within **1.0 dB** of
   the reference.
2. **Size cue.** At each distance, the level difference between the reference disc and
   each comparison disc within **1.5 dB** of the reference's difference. Fifteen pairs.
3. **Decision rule.** The engine is used in exercises only for cases that pass both.
   For a failing case the exercise uses responses precomputed with the wave solver, and
   the failure is recorded in the conformance matrix.

## Predictions, recorded before running

- The 5.1 cm disc will fail metric 1 with first-order edge diffraction at all three
  distances: over the click band its ka runs from 0.9 to 1.9, inside or just above the
  gap found in EXP-0002.
- Discs of 13.5 cm and larger will pass at 66 and 99 cm.
- At 33 cm the larger discs subtend a wide angle (radius up to 0.38 of the distance), so
  waves crossing the disc from rim to rim will be strong; whether first-order
  diffraction passes there is not predicted.

## Results (2026-11-16, corrected 2026-11-17)

Engine: increment 1 of `soundsight.acoustics.engine` (specular reflection plus
first-order edge diffraction; each disc as a 96-sided polygon of equal area). Reference:
the wave solver at 12 to 15 cells per radius and at least 10 cells per wavelength at
4 kHz, as pre-registered. Levels are averages over 41 fixed frequencies from 2 to 4 kHz
(amendment 2). Every number is regenerated by
`uv run python tools/experiments/exp0003.py --check`, which a CI workflow runs weekly and
on every change to the acoustics code; stored values are in `EXP-0003-results.json`,
together with those of the first run.

### Amendment 1, 2026-11-16: reading of the decision rule

The rule says the engine is used "only for cases that pass both" metrics. The reference
disc takes part in every pair, so read literally it would fail metric 2 at every distance
because of the 5.1 cm pair, although its own level is within 0.23 dB. The rule is applied
per **trial**: a pair of discs at one distance uses the engine only if both discs pass
metric 1 and the pair passes metric 2. This reading was recorded after the results were
seen; under either reading the same trials use the wave solver.

### Amendment 2, 2026-11-17: frequency sampling of the level metric

The protocol defined the level as an average "over 2 to 4 kHz" but not at which
frequencies. The first run averaged over the wave solver's FFT bins: 7 to 17 per case, at
positions that depend on the length of the recording. While preparing EXP-0004, a
self-check between two excitations of the same reference disagreed by 1.3 dB, and the
cause was traced to this: on a steep spectrum (a small disc's echo rises about 20 dB per
octave) the average depends on where the bins fall. The recordings themselves were
complete: evaluated at fixed frequencies, levels changed by less than 0.001 dB when the
recording was made up to 20 times longer.

Correction: every level is now the average over 41 equally spaced frequencies from 2 to
4 kHz, each an exact transform of the recordings. Thresholds, workload, reference
resolution and decision rule are unchanged. Effect: reference levels moved by up to
0.54 dB and engine errors by up to 0.38 dB; one verdict changed (the 9.0 cm disc at 33 cm,
from -1.01 to -0.92 dB, now passes). Both runs are stored. The same defect affected the
band levels of the conformance tests, which now use fixed frequencies too; no verdict
there changed.

### What this shows, and what it does not

- The engine is accurate enough for the larger discs of the published task, and the
  exercise pack is not blocked: every trial has a validated echo.
- First-order diffraction is not enough for discs one or two wavelengths around. The next
  engine increment is chosen by EXP-0004 and judged by re-running this experiment
  unchanged.
- The reference is a simulation. Agreement with it says nothing yet about recorded
  echoes, and the task's real discs were foam board on a pole, two at a time.
